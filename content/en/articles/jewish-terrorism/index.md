Many people have come to feel disgust toward Muslims, treating them as enemies and pursuing them, and it is noticeable that the Muslim and the Arab suffer, at the world's airports and ports, from an excess of procedures and scrutiny, out of fear that terrorists might enter and tamper with the security of states.

Muslims generally do not deny the existence of terrorists who use the Islamic religion to justify their misdeeds, and it is these people who distort the image of Islam and Muslims, entrenching a prevailing impression that Islam is a bloody religion, dealing with others without mercy. Perhaps ordinary people, at the global level, cannot be blamed, since they are influenced by the media, political statements, and state programs, taking on board what media figures and politicians say — but the blame falls on those who describe themselves as intellectuals and academics and do not read to ascertain the truth of the matter.

There are European and American media outlets, such as Charlie Hebdo, that never tire of heaping insults and abuse on Muslims, stripping them of their humanity, and they certainly do so deliberately, egged on by others to continue their attack on Islam and the Arabs, the Zionists and their allies especially.

**Torah Thought and Humanity**

The Torah speaks of relations with non-Hebrews, or other peoples, at three levels: the sons of our master Abraham from his wife Hagar; the local inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites especially; and other peoples outside the Holy Land, such as the Egyptians and the Ammonites. It is clear from the outset that the Torah colors this relationship with a superior outlook, placing Jews atop the mountain while placing others at the bottom of the valley. This is a view governed by the notion of the Hebrews' monopoly over the Lord, and His denial of His lordship to others.

They are God's chosen people, surrounded by His care and attention, while the others are mere wandering peoples enjoying no elevated privileges or respect. This thinking is consistent with the prevailing Hebrew belief that the world is divided into two parts: a people possessing God, His grace, and His love, and other bestial, savage peoples outside the Lord's care.

The Torah forbids Hebrews from mixing with other peoples, because these peoples are impure and contemptible, mere animals in human form, and since the Hebrews are God's chosen people, the chosen must not mix with the impure. For this reason, the Torah sets out the reason God created the other peoples: to be subjugated in defeat before the Children of Israel when the Israelites are obedient to the Lord and applying His commandments, and to be used by the Lord to inflict defeats on the Children of Israel when they disobey Him. The other peoples exist only so that the Hebrews may delight in victory over them, or so that God may punish the Hebrews through them.

"The Torah forbids Hebrews from mixing with other peoples, because these peoples are impure and contemptible, mere animals in human form, and since the Hebrews are God's chosen people, the chosen must not mix with the impure."

As for the levels of dealing with other peoples, according to the Torah's texts, they are confined to the following:

1. Killing: The Lord of the Children of Israel prefers the killing of other peoples and ridding the world of their impurity and filth. The Lord repeatedly commanded the Children of Israel to carry out total destruction of others, according to the Torah's texts. For instance, the Lord commanded the Hebrew army, when it entered Jericho in the time of Joshua son of Nun, to devote to destruction every living soul in it ("devote to destruction" meaning to kill). The Lord commanded them to kill all the people, including women and children, and commanded the killing of animals and livestock, the burning of crops, and the demolition of houses. The Torah boasts that the Hebrew army killed the inhabitants of the town of Ai, located between Jericho and Ramallah, whose population then numbered — according to the Torah — 12,000 souls. In several places, the Torah indicates the Lord commanding killing and slaughter.

2. Expulsion: The second preference of the Torah's Lord is the expulsion of people from the places where the Hebrews are present. The presence of other peoples among the Hebrews negatively affects the Hebrews, afflicting them with impurity and defilement, and impure peoples must not remain among the blessed, sanctified chosen. It is also likely that other peoples would tempt God's chosen people, turning them away from worshipping the Lord and into disobedience and sin. Other peoples are astray, and constantly work to lead others astray as well.

Did not the Jewish rabbi Ovadia Yosef call for the expulsion of Palestinians from Palestine because they are impure and "sons of snakes," as he put it? To this day, Israel's policy rests on ridding itself of Palestinians, one way or another, and religious Jews consider the presence of Palestinians on the Promised Land — as they describe it — a great sin the Lord will punish them for.

3. Enslavement: The Lord commands the enslavement of other peoples if expulsion is not possible. The Torah tells God's chosen people that the Lord will make the other peoples their servants, letting them use them however they wish, according to their own desires.

Israel and Zionism have practiced these levels against the Palestinians. They practiced expulsion, too, through massacres.

As for the Palestinians who remained on the land of Palestine, whether in the part occupied in 1948 or the part occupied in 1967, they were enslaved by the Israelis on farms and Israeli facilities, becoming important instruments of production in the Israeli economy.
